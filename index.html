01 Where it began

“The Analytical Engine weaves algebraical patterns just as the Jacquard loom weaves flowers and leaves.”

Ada Lovelace, 1843

The first programmable machine was a loom.

In 1804, Joseph-Marie Jacquard built a loom that followed instructions punched as holes into cards. Ada Lovelace saw the same idea could work with numbers, and wrote what many call the first computer program. Every computer since comes from that loom.

We work in the same spirit: code, design, data and AI brought together into one product that works as a whole.

Look closer: the holes in each card spell a word in computer code. The card on top right now reads AURORA TECH.

04 Our product · Free open beta

Snapplyze

Your personal AI data analyst.

Most analytics tools show you numbers. Snapplyze tells you what they mean, and what to do next.

Join the free beta How it works

The problem

The data is there. The understanding isn't.

Dashboards are made for analysts, but most people who open them aren't analysts. Founders, marketers and shop owners look at dozens of charts and still can't tell if business is going well, what changed, why, or what to do next.

Hiring an analyst fixes this, but most small businesses can't afford one. So the most valuable part, explaining what the numbers mean, never happens. Snapplyze fills that gap.

How it works

  1. 01 ConnectConnect your data sources when you sign up, with no setup wizard. Your history loads in the background, and recent numbers are ready right away.
  2. 02 AskAsk in plain words: “How did we do last week?” “Why did sales drop?” “Where should I spend my next $1,000?”
  3. 03 UnderstandEvery answer explains its reasoning and ends with a clear next step. It remembers how you measure things, what matters to you, and past events that affect your numbers.
  4. 04 Get a summaryYour dashboard is organised by business question: sales, traffic, conversions, customers, retention and products. One click turns any section into a short written summary.
Snapplyze · AI analyst Example
Why did sales drop last week?

Sales fell 18% compared with the week before, while visits stayed the same, so this isn't a traffic problem. The drop is almost all in mobile checkout (−31%), and it started on Tuesday, the day the new checkout page went live. Desktop sales didn't change.

Data checkPurchases from iPhones dropped to almost zero on Tuesday. That usually means the tracking broke, not that sales stopped.

What to doCheck the purchase tracking on the new mobile checkout first. If your payment provider also shows fewer orders, switch back to the old checkout step.

What makes it different

It explains, not just shows.

Every answer ends with a recommendation, something specific to check, or an honest “nothing to do here.” You're never left alone with a chart.

It's honest about data.

It checks the numbers first, flags broken tracking, and tells you what is measured and what is a guess. It never makes up a number to sound sure.

Just ask, like a colleague.

No dashboards to set up and no reports to build. You ask a question the way you'd ask a teammate.

It knows which number to trust.

When Google and Meta disagree (and they always do), it explains why, and tells you which number to use for which decision.
